Zero to 10,000 Users:
Building Fitxn from a Blank Canvas

01

Overview

Fitxn is a health and fitness SaaS platform built to connect individuals with fitness services and businesses. Think of it as an operating layer for the fitness economy, a single platform where users discover, book, and track fitness services, and where fitness businesses manage clients and operations.

I joined as a founding team member on a 6-month contract in a hybrid role that touched every layer of the product: UX/UI design, brand identity, and go-to-market. Not a consultant, not a vendor, a builder embedded in the team at the stage when nothing existed except an idea and a founding team with the technical foundation to execute it.

02

The Problem

Fitxn was entering a saturated market, fitness apps, booking tools, and wellness platforms are everywhere. The challenge wasn't building a technically capable product. It was building one that felt worth switching to, and telling a story that made the switch feel obvious.

At founding stage, the team had none of the foundational layers needed to go to market:

  • No visual identity , no brand language, color system, or design direction; the product had no face
  • No UX system , no established design patterns, component logic, or interaction model; every screen was a blank page
  • No positioning clarity , who was Fitxn for? Individual users? Businesses? Both? The value proposition was undefined
  • No acquisition strategy , the founding team had deep technical expertise but limited capacity for marketing and growth thinking
  • Crowded market with low switching incentive , the fitness tech space is full of established players; differentiation had to be earned at the product and messaging layer simultaneously
03

The Approach

The first principle I operated by: understand the product deeply before building anything for it. Bad design and bad marketing both share the same root cause , building before understanding. I didn't start with screens or content. I started with questions.

Discovery & Jobs-to-be-Done

Ran structured discovery sessions with the founding team to map out the core user jobs Fitxn was solving. What does a user actually hire this product to do? What makes them switch from their current solution?

Dual-Audience Positioning

Defined a dual-sided positioning strategy: individual users (fitness-seekers) and fitness businesses (gyms, trainers, studios). Different value propositions, different conversion paths , both served by the same product.

Design System Before Screens

Built the Figma design system and brand identity before touching individual product screens. A coherent system produces consistent output faster than designing screen by screen.

Parallel GTM + Product Track

Ran product design and go-to-market in parallel, not sequentially. Early GTM content informed what product flows needed to be prioritized. Each track fed the other.

04

What I Did

My scope was intentionally broad , founding-stage work requires operating across multiple functions simultaneously. Here's what I owned end-to-end:

  • Brand Identity System , created the full visual identity from scratch: logo direction, color palette, typography system, iconography principles, and brand guidelines the team could use independently
  • UX/UI Design System in Figma , built the complete component library, interaction patterns, and layout system that underpinned every product screen
  • Core Product Screen Design , designed all primary user-facing flows including onboarding, discovery, booking, and business-side management views, with a focus on reducing friction at every step
  • Go-to-Market Strategy , developed the acquisition positioning, channel strategy, and content framework for both the individual user and business partner audiences
  • Early-Stage Content & Landing Pages , created marketing content and acquisition landing pages that communicated Fitxn's value to both target audiences and drove early sign-ups
  • B2B Outreach Framework , built the outreach strategy and materials for acquiring fitness businesses as partners , the supply side of the platform
05

Execution

The hardest execution challenge in a two-sided platform is sequencing. You need users for the platform to be valuable for businesses, and you need businesses for the platform to be valuable for users. Neither audience signs up for an empty marketplace.

My approach was to build demand from both sides simultaneously while using early business partners as social proof for individual users , and individual user interest as leverage in B2B conversations.

Key Execution Insight

In two-sided platforms, the chicken-and-egg problem is usually solved by manufacturing one side's confidence before the other side arrives. We led with fitness businesses , they provide visible proof that the platform is real and functional. Individual users join a platform that already has supply. We built the supply side first, then opened the demand side.

  • Designed onboarding flows that minimized time-to-value , users experienced the product's core utility within the first 60 seconds
  • Developed landing pages with distinct conversion paths for individual users and fitness business owners
  • Built a content strategy that produced social proof at scale , user testimonials, business partner stories, and outcome-focused case content
  • Maintained design coherence across product, marketing, and brand touchpoints so every interaction reinforced the same identity

Key Insight

The Real Unlock

Design and marketing are often treated as separate functions at early-stage companies. The most effective founding-stage operators collapse that distinction. A product that looks trustworthy converts better on the marketing side. Marketing copy that matches the product's visual language reduces cognitive friction at onboarding. When one person owns both surfaces, the experience becomes coherent end-to-end , and coherent experiences convert. That's what I built at Fitxn.

08

What This Project Shows

  • Founding-stage range , I operated across UX design, brand identity, content strategy, and GTM simultaneously in a high-ambiguity environment
  • Two-sided platform thinking , understood the sequencing and incentive structure of building supply and demand simultaneously
  • Systems over deliverables , built a design system and GTM playbook that outlasted the contract; the team didn't lose momentum when I left
  • Product and marketing coherence , maintained a single identity across product screens, brand assets, and marketing copy , rare when one person owns all three
  • Speed with quality , delivered 10k users and 50+ business partners within 6 months from a zero starting point, without sacrificing design integrity
